Explained simply. Imagine a famous author who writes brilliant letters but is painfully slow: for every single word, they have to walk down to a huge library, look things up, and walk back. Now give them a quick assistant who just guesses the next few words. The author doesn't write the words one at a time anymore. They read the assistant's guess for the whole line in one trip to the library, tick off the words they would have written themselves, and cross out the first one they wouldn't have. Everything before the crossed-out word is kept for free.

The clever part is how the author decides what to keep. The rule is tuned so that the finished letter reads exactly as if the author had written every word alone, no matter how good or bad the assistant's guesses are. Good guesses just mean fewer library trips. That is the whole trick: same words, less waiting.

Everyday example

The letter so far says "The cat sat on the". The assistant guesses the next three words: "mat", "and", "purred". The author makes one trip to the library and checks all three guesses together. "mat" is something they would happily write, so it stays. "and" is not what they had in mind, so it gets crossed out, and "purred" goes with it because it was a guess built on "and". The author then writes their own word in the crossed-out spot, say a full stop, and the letter now ends "...on the mat." That is two new words for a single trip to the library. Had all three guesses been fine, the author would have added one more word of their own, for four words in one trip.
