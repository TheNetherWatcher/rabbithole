Explain it simply

Think of a map app. Zoomed out, a city is a grey blob; zoomed in, you see every house. The app does not keep one giant picture and shrink it. It keeps the city cut into small tiles, and for every tile it keeps cruder versions too. You only load the tiles you can see, at just the sharpness your screen can show.

Nanite does this for 3D models. A statue with a million tiny triangles is chopped into little patches of 128 triangles. Each patch has coarser stand-ins. Every frame the GPU asks, for each patch: "if I used the coarse version, would anyone notice on screen?" If not, it uses the coarse one. The trick that makes it work is that neighbouring patches always agree on the exact points along their shared edges, so a coarse patch next to a fine patch still fits like a jigsaw piece, with no gaps.

Everyday example

A game has a stone dragon statue made of about 1,000,000 triangles. Stand 2 metres away: the statue fills the screen, so you need nearly all the detail; the game draws the fine patches facing you and skips the back of the statue. Walk 200 metres away: the statue is a 30-pixel smudge. The game swaps each patch for its coarsest stand-in, maybe a few hundred triangles in total, and nobody can tell. Walk back slowly: patches near the middle of the statue get sharper first, one patch at a time, and no seam ever opens up because the borders between patches never moved.
