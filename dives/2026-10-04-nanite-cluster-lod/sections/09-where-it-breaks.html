Overdraw and thin geometry. Simplification removes few triangles from layered or holey meshes, so many depth layers must be drawn before occlusion helps. One article claims up to 2× worse than expected (80.lv, blog-grade).
Aggregates (leaves, grass, hair). Disconnected parts do not collapse into a plane, so thin features vanish or stay dense. Epic's answer in the UE 5.8 docs is Assemblies, Voxels and Skinning (verified).
WPO and animation. Cluster bounds are static, so displaced vertices can be culled wrongly and need a max-WPO-distance setting. Animation also invalidates cached virtual-shadow-map pages (forums and blogs, secondary).
Masked materials need separate raster bins and a per-pixel alpha test inside the raster loop, which is costly.
No translucency, and deferred only. Forward VR and mobile are out (Meta docs and an Arm blog, secondary).
Ray tracing. Nanite clusters do not map directly to hardware BLAS. Cluster acceleration structures (RTX Mega Geometry, 2025) are the hardware answer (verified that they exist).
Streaming. An undersized pool thrashes, and fast cameras cause pop-in. An SSD is effectively required (secondary).
Subpixel shimmer. Sub-pixel triangles cause temporal instability and rely on TSR or TAA (Epic thin-geometry docs, secondary).
Misconceptions. "Free LODs": the cost moves to material shading, shadows and memory. "Always faster": measured results are mixed. One forum test found Nanite about 1.5 ms slower than an overdraw-optimised LOD scene; another found 55–58 FPS against 47–49 on an RTX 3090 (secondary).
