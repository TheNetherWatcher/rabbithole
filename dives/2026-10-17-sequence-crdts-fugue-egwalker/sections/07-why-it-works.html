The core insight: concurrent edits only conflict about where. Give every character an identity, and location becomes a relationship between identities that every replica can resolve identically. The remaining design freedom is the tie-break, and that single rule decides both convergence and readability.

Remove each piece and see what breaks

RemoveWhat breaks
Unique IDs (use indexes)Concurrent edits shift each other: Hell!o vs Hello!. This is why OT must transform indexes pairwise.
The ID tie-breakTwo replicas can order concurrent siblings differently, so they diverge permanently.
TombstonesAn operation naming a deleted anchor has nothing to attach to. The Fugue paper notes tombstones can't be dropped because they may be ancestors of live or concurrent nodes.
Subtree structure (per-character tie-break, as in RGA)Backward-typed runs interleave: xaybzc in my simulation. Convergence is intact; readability is not.
Prepare/effect split (Eg-walker)An event can't be interpreted in the version its author saw while being applied to the current one; you are back to pairwise transformation.
Clearing at critical versions (Eg-walker)The temporary state lives for the whole history. The paper's Fig. 9 shows merge time on S1 rising from 1.8 ms to 9.8 ms and on S3 from 3.6 ms to 24.4 ms.

Read the bars as “with the optimisation / without”. A2 (no critical versions at all) barely changes: 23.5 ms vs 23.7 ms. That is the paper's own explanation for where clearing matters: long stretches of sequential editing.
