“A sequence CRDT gives every character a unique ID, so ‘insert after this character’ means the same thing on every replica and all copies converge.”

True, and it hides five things:

  1. Convergence is the easy part. The hard part is the tie-break for two inserts after the same character. A rule can converge perfectly and still weave two typed words together.
  2. Deleted text is never really gone. A tombstone must stay, because a concurrent insert may name it as its anchor.
  3. The metadata is bigger than the text. In the Fugue paper's benchmark, a 105 kB document saves to 168 kB.
  4. You may not need the CRDT most of the time. Eg-walker keeps only an edit log and builds a CRDT just for the stretch where people truly overlapped.
  5. “Same text everywhere” is not “what the users meant.” Convergence does not preserve intention; the interleaving literature exists because of this gap.
