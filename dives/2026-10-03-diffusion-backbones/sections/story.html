Every image starts as static. One denoiser cleans it up a little at a time, 28–50 times. Its inside is the backbone, and that is what this page follows.

The naive way. Treat every pixel as a token. Attention over a 256² image then means 65,536 tokens. ADM, a pixel U-Net, costs 1120 GFLOPs per forward pass.

The key idea. Denoise a compressed latent instead. The VAE makes it 8× smaller on each side, and 2×2 patches turn it into tokens. A 1024² image becomes 4,096 tokens.

Text joins. The prompt becomes about 512 text tokens. They sit in the same sequence as the image tokens, so attention sees 4,608 tokens in all.

Double-stream blocks. Flux runs 19 of these. Image and text keep separate weights, but their tokens attend over one shared sequence.

Single-stream blocks. Then 38 more blocks. By now text and image are aligned enough to share one set of weights for the rest of the stack.

Output and loop. The network predicts a velocity, and one small Euler step moves the static toward a picture. The whole stack runs again, 28–50 times per image.

The catch. Attention cost grows with the square of the token count. A 1024² image stays under the crossover. Video does not: a 720p clip has about 119,000 tokens, far past T = 6d.
