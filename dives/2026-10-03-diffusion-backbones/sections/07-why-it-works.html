Why the U-Net worked first:

  • It gives multi-scale receptive fields cheaply: global layout at 8², texture at 64².
  • Attention runs only where it is affordable.
  • Skip connections let high-frequency detail bypass the bottleneck, which is essential at low t.
  • Remove the skips and small models lose detail. SiD2 found small models lose about 0.2 FID without blockwise skips, while large ones do not care (SiD2).

Why DiT won the 2023–26 default:

  1. A one-knob scaling law. Depth, width and patch size map onto Gflops, and Gflops map onto FID (r = −0.93). Bigger models are also more compute-efficient: XL/2 beats XL/4 after about 10¹⁰ training Gflops (DiT).
  2. The LLM stack transfers: FlashAttention, FSDP and sequence parallelism, fp8, RoPE, QK-norm, MoE, torch.compile.
  3. Any-shape inputs are just token sequences: video spacetime patches, variable aspect ratio, multiple images (Flux Kontext concatenates context tokens).
  4. Joint attention gives bidirectional text–image flow. On CC12M, SD3 found that DiT < UViT < CrossDiT < MMDiT (SD3 §5.2).

Each point is one DiT model (sizes S to XL, patch sizes 8 to 2) at 400K training steps. Lower FID goes with more transformer Gflops, on a log axis.
