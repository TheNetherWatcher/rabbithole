On the CPU side the design is settled: Abseil, Rust and Go all adopted group probing. Active work moved to GPUs, concurrent designs with cheap deletes, and theory.

  • Go 1.24 Swiss map, 2025, go.dev blog: production-scale adoption with a directory-of-tables twist. (Performance figures in secondary posts are unverified.)
  • DLHT, Katsarakis, Gavrielatos, Ntarmos, HPDC 2024, arXiv 2406.09986: a concurrent, non-blocking, resizable table using closed addressing with cache-line chaining and immediate reuse of deleted slots; reports over 1.6 billion requests/s. The most direct answer to the tombstone critique.
  • WarpSpeed, McCoy and Pandey, ALENEX 2026, arXiv 2509.16407: a library and benchmark over eight GPU hash table designs with fingerprint metadata and lock-free queries.
  • A Fine-Grained Analysis of SIMD-Based Hash Table Implementations, Nicaud and Rotondo, arXiv preprint (Oct 2026), 2610.02385: analyses SIMD open addressing with Wormald's differential-equation method. Its experiments simulate its own model; it does not benchmark Abseil, hashbrown or Go.
  • Hive Hash Table (arXiv 2510.15095, 2025) and HierarchicalKV (arXiv 2603.17168, 2026): GPU tables reporting 1.5–2× and 1.4× over WarpCore on their own setups; venues unverified.
Not verified: the LLVM FoldingSet Swiss-table RFC (reported ~1.4% clang speedup) comes from a forum snippet only. I found no peer-reviewed 2024–26 critique of Swiss tables, and no VLDB/SIGMOD paper on learned hash functions in the window. Iceberg hashing (Bender et al., 2023) falls just outside it.
