Restaurant order windowKitchen (kernel) and diner (app) share two conveyor belts: orders in (SQ), plates out (CQ). The diner stacks 32 order slips then rings the bell once. The cook grabs whatever's quick (inline), parks slow orders on a side-queue (poll waitlist) or hands them to a prep crew (io-wq).

Breaks where:

The paper's picture of the same idea: the database and the kernel exchange requests through two rings in shared memory, and the submit call crosses the boundary once.

  • The belt carries indexes to slips, not slips (SQ array indirection).
  • The cook may not be asynchronous at all: simple orders finish while you're ringing the bell; the CQE is already there on return.
  • The prep crew = real kernel threads in your process (since 5.12), counted against RLIMIT_NPROC.
  • Once the cook has read a slip, you may reuse the slot, but the kernel may still be using your buffers.
