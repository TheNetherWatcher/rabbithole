Explain it simply

Picture a busy cook who needs things from a helpful pantry assistant. The old way: the cook walks over, asks for one thing, waits, walks back, then repeats for every single item. Most of the time goes into the walking back and forth, not the fetching. io_uring replaces that with two trays on a shared counter: an "orders" tray the cook drops notes into, and a "done" tray the assistant drops results into. The cook stacks up several notes, rings the bell once, and goes back to cooking.

When the bell rings, the assistant first does whatever is within arm's reach right away. Anything that has to wait (a parcel that has not arrived yet, a book in the basement) gets handled without the assistant standing around: either "I'll tap you when it shows up" or "a helper will go fetch it". Results land in the done tray, each with the number from the original note so the cook can match them up. The point: fewer interruptions and many errands in flight at once.

Everyday example

Sam is cooking dinner and writes three notes: (1) "read me the first page of the recipe card", (2) "tell me when the grocery parcel arrives", (3) "bring the big cookbook from the basement". Sam numbers them 1, 2, 3, drops them in the orders tray, and rings the bell once.

The assistant grabs the recipe card from the shelf right there and puts the page in the done tray before Sam has even looked up. For the parcel, nothing has arrived, so the assistant says "I'll flag it when the doorbell goes" and does not wait at the door. The basement book is far away, so a helper is sent. Later Sam glances at the done tray: slip 1 is there already, then slip 3 when the helper returns, then slip 2 when the parcel finally arrives. Sam matches each slip to its note by number. One bell, three errands, no waiting around, and results can come back in any order.
