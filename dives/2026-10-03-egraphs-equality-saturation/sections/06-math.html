6.1 The invariants

Let ≡id be the union-find relation and ≡node the relation "two e-nodes are in the same class". The congruence invariant (egg Def 2.6) says ≡node equals its own congruence closure:

f(a₁,…,aₖ) ≡node f(b₁,…,bₖ)  ⇐  ∀i. find(aᵢ) = find(bᵢ)→ in plain terms: two nodes with the same operator and the same canonical children must be in the same class.

The hashcons invariant (Def 2.7): n ∈ M[a] ⇔ H[canon(n)] = find(a), where canon(f(a₁…aₖ)) = f(find(a₁)…find(aₖ)).

n ∈ M[a]  ⇔  H[canon(n)] = find(a)→ in plain terms: the lookup table is keyed by nodes whose children are representatives, so "have I seen this node?" is one hash probe.

Why this form and not "keep a tree per term": the right-hand side quantifies over classes, so one equality a ≡ b instantly covers every term containing a. That's what makes the representation exponentially compact, and it's also why one merge can cascade.

6.2 Why deferring rebuild wins

Take n terms f1(x), …, fn(x) plus leaves y1..yn, then merge x with each yi (egg §3.2.1). Immediate maintenance repairs x's parent list after every merge: n merges × n parents = O(n²) hashcons updates. Deferred: all n merges go on the worklist, deduplicate to one canonical class, and its n parents are repaired once: O(n). For w chains of depth d whose leaves get merged, immediate costs O(wd) repairs, deferred O(d), because the w upward paths coalesce after dedup.

→ in plain terms: rebuilding pays once per distinct dirty class per iteration, not once per merge.

Measured on egg's 32-test suite: 88× geometric-mean speedup on congruence closure, 21× on whole eqsat, deferred vs immediate rebuilding inside egg (not vs Herbie; that's a common misquote). egg proves rebuilding terminates (Thm 3.1) via a lexicographically decreasing (|incongruent pairs|, |worklist|) measure, but gives no online complexity bound ("likely highly workload dependent").

6.3 AC blowup, derived

Saturate x1 + … + xn (distinct leaves) under commutativity and associativity. Any regrouping and reordering is reachable, so every non-empty subset S of the leaves is the sum of some subterm: 2ⁿ − 1 e-classes. A class for a subset of size k ≥ 2 contains one + node per ordered split of S into two non-empty parts: 2k − 2 of them. Summing:

#(+-nodes) = Σk=2n C(n,k)(2k − 2) = 3n − 2n+1 + 1(the k = 0 and k = 1 terms of the two sums cancel to −1, hence +1). Adding the n leaf nodes gives 3n − 2n+1 + n + 1 e-nodes, matching Rosin, Ullrich and Hack's EGRAPHS 2026 paper.

→ in plain terms: 10 summed variables → 1,023 classes and 57,012 nodes; 20 → over 3.4 billion nodes. Commutativity alone is mild; the pair with associativity is what explodes.

6.4 E-matching as a join

Relational e-matching (Zhang et al., POPL 2022) stores each e-node f(j1..jk) in class i as a tuple R_f(i, j1, …, jk). The pattern f(α, g(α)) becomes the conjunctive query

Q(root, α) ← Rf(root, α, x), Rg(x, α)

Backtracking checks the repeated α last: O(N²) for N matches. A join checks it during the join: O(N). Using Generic Join (worst-case optimal), the time is bounded by the AGM bound; Thm 10 gives O(√(|Q(I)| · ∏ᵢ|Rᵢ|)). The paper reports "over 6 orders of magnitude faster" on deep patterns with repeated variables. The speedup plot below is that evaluation: bars right of the 1× line mean relational e-matching is faster.

→ in plain terms: e-matching is NP-hard in pattern size, but patterns are tiny; in e-graph size, it's polynomial and a database planner does it well.

6.5 Extraction

With a monotone local cost (cost(f(c1..ck)) > every child cost), greedy bottom-up fixpoint is optimal for tree cost (each use counted separately). Under DAG cost (a shared class counted once) it's NP-hard (Stepp 2011 thesis) and inapproximable to any constant factor (Goharshady et al., OOPSLA 2024). The standard exact method is ILP (Tensat):

min Σᵢ cᵢxᵢ   s.t.   Σi∈root xᵢ = 1,   xᵢ ≤ Σj∈m xⱼ  ∀i, ∀m ∈ children(i)plus acyclicity via order variables tₘ: tg(i) − tₘ − ε + A(1 − xᵢ) ≥ 0. In plain terms: pick exactly one node at the root; picking a node forces you to pick some node in each child class; and the picks must not form a loop. The cycle constraint is what makes the ILP slow; Tensat filters cycles during exploration and drops it.
