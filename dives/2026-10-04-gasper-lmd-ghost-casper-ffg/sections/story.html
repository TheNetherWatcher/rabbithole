Every validator follows one head: the branch with the most latest votes beneath it. In a 100-vote committee, the honest votes split 30 to 31 between two forks, so the lead is one vote wide.

The attack. An adversary signs its votes but holds 39 back instead of sending them. Nothing on the chain shows the held votes until they are released.

Without a boost, the naive rule fails. Release all 39 to fork B and B leads by 40. Balancing attacks keep the two forks tied like this, so the head never settles.

The fix: proposer boost. If the slot's block arrives on time, its branch gets a boost worth 40% of one committee, here 40 votes, for that slot only. The attacker still holds its 39.

It holds. The attacker releases 30 votes. B reaches 61 and A keeps 70, so the on-time block stays head. The boost is what decides it.

The limit. Nine more votes make it a 70 to 70 tie, and the root name breaks the tie. From 41 released votes, B wins outright. The boost is a fixed 40%, not a guarantee.
