Three guests are on a list, and each one gets three slots on a board of six. Each guest's three slots must XOR to a short code. The job is to solve all three at once, without trying every combination. The way in is peeling.

The naive way. Guess slot values at random. A guest's check then passes by luck 1 time in 8, for 3-bit codes. Worse, Ana and Ben share slot A0, so fixing one guest breaks the other.

Key idea: find a lonely slot. C0 is touched by Ana alone. Park Ana on a stack and set her aside. Her slot C0 will absorb her code later.

Peel again. With Ana parked, A1 is touched by Cy alone, so Cy goes on the stack. Ben is the last guest left, and no unparked guest touches his slots.

All three are parked. The stack gives the order, so the table can be solved. Each guest leaves through a slot that no guest still on the board uses.

Fill in reverse. Ben, parked last, goes first. His slot A0 becomes 3, his code, because his other two slots are 0.

Then Cy and Ana. Cy's A1 becomes 6. Ana's C0 becomes 6, so her three slots XOR to 5. Each slot is written once, so the guests already set keep working.

Check. Ana: 3 ⊕ 0 ⊕ 6 = 5. Ben and Cy pass too. A stranger lands on a random value, so an 8-bit code lets one through 1 time in 256.

The numbers at scale. A random 3-way hypergraph peels only above 1.22 slots per key, so an XOR filter uses about 1.23. Binary fuse windows cut that to 1.125. The cards below cover the math, the knobs and where it breaks.
