Think of a bulletin board of sticky notes. Each guest has three notes assigned, and the notes must XOR to the guest's code. Filling the board means solving every guest's equation at once. Peeling means repeatedly setting aside a guest who owns a private note, then filling the board in reverse so that the private note absorbs the slack.

The analogy breaks on sharing. The board has only about 1.23 slots per guest while each guest touches 3, so slots are heavily shared. Solvability is not a local guarantee. It comes from a global property of the random hypergraph: no dense core below roughly 82% load.
