A random 4 KB update to a B-tree touches a leaf page: ~1 random read + 1 random write, with write amp up to page-size / entry-size from rewriting whole pages (illustrative arithmetic: 4096 / 100 ≈ 40×; not a measured figure). An LSM instead batches: buffer B·P entries, write once sequentially. But now a key's versions can be in the memtable and in any run. Without merging, runs grow without bound and lookup cost grows with them; obsolete versions and tombstones never die. Compaction bounds that, at the price of rewriting data.

The question: how many times should each byte be rewritten, and how many runs may a lookup face?
