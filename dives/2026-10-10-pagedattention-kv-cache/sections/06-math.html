6.1 Footprint and batch size

T = M / b,   Ncontig = ⌊T / Lmax⌋,   Npaged ≈ T / E[ B·⌈ℓ/B⌉ ]→ in plain terms: contiguous pays for the worst case; paging pays for the actual average plus at most one block per sequence.

Back-of-envelope (not measured): A100-80GB, Llama-3-8B FP16, utilization 0.92, ~16 GB weights, ~2 GB activations, so M ≈ 55 GB and T ≈ 420k slots. With Lmax = 8192, contiguous fits 51 sequences; paged at mean length 700 fits about 590, before compute and max_num_seqs caps kick in.

6.2 Internal fragmentation

w = B·⌈ℓ/B⌉ − ℓ ∈ [0, B−1],   E[w] = (B−1)/2,   waste ≈ (B−1) / (2·E[ℓ])→ in plain terms: B = 16 with 600-token sequences wastes about 1.25%; B = 512 wastes about 42%.

Why not B = 1: per-block overhead (an indirect lookup, less coalescing) scales with ℓ/B. Total cost is roughly α(B−1)/2 + βℓ/B, minimized near B* ≈ √(2βℓ/α). This cost model is illustrative, not from the paper. The paper's empirical best was 16–128 on ShareGPT, with 16 as the default.

6.3 Block-wise softmax is exact

o = (Σt est vt) / (Σt est),   st = q·kt/√d→ in plain terms: the output is a weighted average of values, and the normalizer runs over all tokens.

Per block j, keep mj = max s, lj = Σ es−mj and aj = Σ es−mj v. Merging two blocks:

m = max(m1, m2);   l = em1−m l1 + em2−m l2;   a = em1−m a1 + em2−m a2;   o = a / l→ in plain terms: each block reports its local max, local sum and local weighted value; rescaling puts them on a common scale, so the result equals contiguous attention up to float rounding. Paging is lossless.

Why this form: per-block softmaxes can't be averaged because the normalizer is global. Subtracting the max prevents FP16 overflow (e11.1 > 65504). The paper's Eq. 4 writes the global denominator; FlashAttention and FlashInfer kernels use the running-max form.

6.4 Swap vs recompute

tswap ≈ 2ℓb / BWpcie + (per-transfer overhead × blocks);   trecompute ≈ 2Pℓ / Feff→ in plain terms: swap pays PCIe bytes plus a fixed cost per block; recompute pays prefill FLOPs.

Back-of-envelope for Llama-3-8B with ℓ = 1000: 131 MB, about 5 ms each way at ~25 GB/s, vs 1.6e13 FLOP, about 50–100 ms. Swap looks cheaper per byte, but per-block overhead dominates at small B. Paper (Fig. 19, OPT-13B): swap is bad at small blocks, recompute is flat across block sizes, the two are comparable at B = 16–64, and recompute never cost more than about 20% over swap. vLLM V1 defaults to recompute.
