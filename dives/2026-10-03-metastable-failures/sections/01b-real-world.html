What it is, in one paragraph

A metastable failure, in the words of the paper that named it, occurs "in open systems with an uncontrolled source of load where a trigger causes the system to enter a bad state that persists even when the trigger is removed. In this state the goodput … is unusably low, and there is a sustaining effect, often involving work amplification or decreased overall efficiency, that prevents the system from leaving the bad state" (Bronson et al., HotOS'21). The name comes from physics: a ball sitting in a shallow dip is stable until a push sends it over the lip, after which it settles somewhere worse and won't come back by itself.

Why it matters

  • Metastable failures are the long outages. In the OSDI'22 study of 22 incidents, outages lasted 1.5 to 73.53 hours, and 4–10 hours was the most common range. The study also found that at least 4 of 15 major AWS outages in the previous decade were metastable (Huang et al., OSDI'22).
  • The root cause gets misdiagnosed. "It is common for an outage that involves a metastable failure to be initially blamed on the trigger, but the true root cause is the sustaining effect" (Bronson). Teams fix the bad deploy, the outage repeats with a different trigger, and the loop was never touched.
  • The loops are built from features that help on a normal day: retries, caches, failover, aggressive timeouts, connection reuse. You add them for reliability and efficiency, and they make up the trap.
  • Before the name existed the pattern was already known as congestion collapse. Nagle, RFC 896 (1984): "This condition is stable. Once the saturation point has been reached … the network will continue to operate in a degraded condition." Jacobson's 1988 TCP fixes (exponential retransmit backoff, slow start, AIMD) were the networking cure. Distributed services rebuilt the same disease one layer up.

How people defend against it (what you actually configure)

// gRPC service config: retries + retry throttling (gRPC proposal A6)
{"methodConfig":[{"name":[{"service":"pkg.Svc"}],
  "retryPolicy":{"maxAttempts":4,"initialBackoff":"0.1s","maxBackoff":"1s",
                 "backoffMultiplier":2,"retryableStatusCodes":["UNAVAILABLE"]}}],
 "retryThrottling":{"maxTokens":10,"tokenRatio":0.1}}

Each server name has a token count starting at maxTokens. A failure takes away 1, a success adds tokenRatio, and retries stop while the count is ≤ maxTokens/2. Typical defaults elsewhere:

  • Envoy retry_budget: budget_percent 20%, min_retry_concurrency 3. Envoy recommends budgets over the static max_retries (default 3) (Envoy docs).
  • Finagle RetryBudget: percentCanRetry 0.2, minRetriesPerSec 10, ttl 10 s (source).
  • AWS SDKs (standard mode): 3 attempts and a 500-token retry quota. botocore/Go v2 charge 5 tokens per retry and 10 per timeout retry. An opt-in 2026 update (AWS_NEW_RETRIES_2026=true) charges 14 per transient retry so the quota engages sooner (AWS blog, botocore).
  • Google SRE: at most 3 attempts per request, per-client retries below 10% of requests, retry only at the layer directly above the failure, and return "overloaded; don't retry" upward (SRE book).
  • Backoff: full jitter, sleep = random(0, min(cap, base·2^attempt)) (AWS Architecture blog, 2015).

Use it

  • Ratio retry budget on every RPC client
  • Deadline propagation + drop expired at dequeue
  • Adaptive LIFO/CoDel on queue-heavy servers
  • Singleflight / leases on hot-key miss paths
  • Headroom below Cstable for critical control planes

Beware / skip

  • Fixed max_retries per layer: multiplies across the stack
  • Backoff alone: defers load but doesn't reduce it
  • Circuit breakers on sharded backends: partial → total failure
  • Deep health checks: remove capacity under load
  • Autoscaling as the cure: too slow, wrong signal

Who should care

Backend engineers who write retry and timeout code. SRE/infra owners of load balancers, meshes and autoscalers. Database and cache owners, since a cold cache is the classic sustaining effect. Platform and control-plane teams: the AWS 2025 outage was a control-plane recovery path collapsing. Anyone serving LLMs, where KV-cache preemption plus client retries looks like the same loop (still a research gap, see §11b).
