The math has held. Every practical key recovery so far came through implementations or composition, and the FO re-encryption step is what turns small leaks into full key recovery.

KyberSlash 1 & 2 (Dec 2023 – Jan 2024; TCHES 2025, Best Paper). - The reference code computed t = (((t << 1) + KYBER_Q/2)/KYBER_Q) & 1 in poly_tomsg (KyberSlash1, during decryption) and similar /KYBER_Q lines in poly_compress/polyvec_compress (KyberSlash2, during encryption, which decaps runs as re-encryption on the secret m′). - Whether this leaks depends on the compiler and CPU. gcc -Os on x86 emits DIV, and on Raspberry Pi 2 it calls __divsi3, which branches on its input. DIV latency depends on the operands: on Zen 2 it is 1 cycle slower once the numerator reaches 8192. - Chosen ciphertexts steer the secret numerator across those thresholds. KyberSlash2 recovered keys in minutes on a Cortex-M4; KyberSlash1 took hours on a Raspberry Pi 2. - Found by Cryspen while writing verified Rust, and independently by Bernstein via SUPERCOP. - The fix is a multiply-and-shift t = ((t<<1)+1665)*80635 >> 28 & 1. experiments.py checks it equals the division for all 3329 inputs. - More than 15 libraries were patched (liboqs, AWS-LC, Botan, pqm4, PQClean, zig std, …). Never vulnerable: BoringSSL, Go's mlkem768, libjade, the pq-crystals AVX2 code. - Follow-up: the multiply-and-shift fix still leaks through power, giving SPA-assisted key recovery in about 30 s on a Cortex-M4 (ePrint 2024/2051). - Sources: kyberslash.cr.yp.to, ePrint 2024/1049.

Clangover (Jun 2024, CVE-2024-37880). - poly_frommsg used the branch-free idiom mask = -((msg[i]>>j)&1). Clang 15–18 recognized that the value is 0 or 1 and compiled it into a branch on secret m′, at -Os, -O1, and -O2/-O3 with -fno-vectorize. - That gives a timing plaintext-checking oracle and ML-KEM-512 key recovery in 5–10 minutes. - Fixed with an empty inline-asm value barrier. liboqs: CVE-2024-36405. - Lesson: constant-time C is not preserved by the compiler. - Source: clangover.

Re-encryption comparison oracles. - A memcmp that exits early in FO decaps leaks how many bytes matched. Guo–Johansson–Nilsson broke FrodoKEM with about 2^30 decapsulations this way (CRYPTO 2020). - Power and EM leakage from the SHA-3 calls during re-encryption is a generic plaintext-checking oracle ("Curse of Re-encryption", TCHES 2022). - Fault injection that skips one instruction in decoding recovered a Kyber512 key from about 6,500 faulty decapsulations (Pessl–Prokop TCHES 2021). - 2026 logic bug: wolfSSL's AVX2 mlkem_cmp_avx2 compared only 1536 of the 1568 ciphertext bytes of ML-KEM-1024, so tampering with the last 32 bytes skipped implicit rejection. That is a plaintext-checking oracle needing about 350 queries and no timing at all. Reported as CVE-2026-10097 (OpenCVE; I have only seen this in aggregator listings, not NVD).

Side channels on protected code (2025). - Chosen-ciphertext power attacks recover keys even from shuffled implementations: about 1,072 queries with an MLP classifier (ePrint 2025/1956). - Template attacks on base multiplication need at most 40 traces (ePrint 2025/1577). - Masking has to cover compress/decompress, the hashes and the comparison, not only the NTT.

Binding failures. - An expanded dk is (ŝ, ek, H(ek), z), and decaps doesn't check that these are consistent. With a malicious key you can make two different ciphertexts, or two public keys, decapsulate to the same K. - So ML-KEM is not MAL-BIND-K-CT / MAL-BIND-K-PK (Schmieg, ePrint 2024/523; Cremers–Dax–Medinger CCS 2024). It is LEAK-BIND for honestly generated keys. - Fixes: seed-only private keys (64 B d‖z, now RFC 9935's recommendation) and protocols that hash ct and pk into the final key.

Conformance gaps. - Symbolic Software's 2026 "Crucible" suite found that pq-crystals ref, PQClean and pqcrypto skip the FIPS modulus check and accept coefficient 3329 (blog). - Bouncy Castle 1.80 accepted a 2399-byte dk.

Deployment breakage. - The ClientHello with a hybrid share is larger than one MTU. Middleboxes and servers that call read() once and expect the whole ClientHello broke when Chrome 124 shipped Kyber (tldr.fail). - Go keeps GODEBUG=tlsmlkem=0 as an escape hatch.

Misconceptions

  • "Kyber is quantum-proof." It rests on the assumption that MLWE is hard. In April 2024 Yilei Chen's claimed polynomial-time quantum LWE algorithm caused real alarm until a bug in Step 9 was found and the claim was withdrawn on Apr 18 (ePrint 2024/555). That episode is the argument for hybrids.
  • "ML-KEM is a drop-in replacement for DH." A KEM isn't a NIKE. There is no static-static agreement, no DH ratchet and no implicit authentication. Signal, Noise, WireGuard and EDHOC need redesigns (draft-sfluhrer-cfrg-ml-kem-security-considerations).
  • "Decryption failures don't matter at 2^-139." Failures are why the parameters look the way they do. Failure boosting means finding the first one makes the next ones much cheaper (D'Anvers et al. 2020).
  • "You can use K-PKE if you're careful." FIPS 203 says it "shall not be used as a stand-alone scheme". With reused keys it leaks s.
  • "Round-3 Kyber == ML-KEM." They aren't wire-compatible: the FO variant changed, H(m) was removed and domain separation was added. That is why TLS needed a new codepoint.
