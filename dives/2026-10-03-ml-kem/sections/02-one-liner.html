"ML-KEM is a lattice-based KEM whose security comes from Module-LWE: a public key is As + e, and the small error e makes it hard to recover s."

What that sentence leaves out: 1. The error that protects the secret also corrupts the message. Why does decryption work anyway, and how was 2^-164.8 computed? (§6) 2. The lattice core is only CPA-secure. On its own it falls to chosen-ciphertext key recovery. The Fujisaki–Okamoto transform with re-encryption and implicit rejection fixes that, and it also makes every side channel in decryption a key-recovery oracle. (§5, §7, §9) 3. The NTT is part of the format, not just an optimization. The matrix A is sampled directly in the NTT domain, and q = 3329 was picked so a 7-layer incomplete NTT exists. (§5, §6) 4. "Compression" removes ciphertext bits and adds noise on purpose. The noise budget is shared between sampled noise and rounding noise. (§6, §8) 5. The shared key doesn't commit to everything you would assume. ML-KEM isn't MAL-BIND-K-CT (an attacker with a malicious key can make two ciphertexts give the same K), which is why hybrids hash the transcript. (§9, §11)
