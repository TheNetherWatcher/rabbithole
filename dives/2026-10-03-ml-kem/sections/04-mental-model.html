A noisy dial with a secret calibration. Each coefficient is a dial with q = 3329 positions. The public key is a set of dials (t = As + e) that look random unless you know the small calibration s. To send a bit, the sender adds a random-looking combination of public dials (tᵀy) plus small noise, then turns the dial by half a revolution for a 1. The receiver can compute nearly the same random combination from the other half of the ciphertext (sᵀu). Subtracting it leaves "half a turn or none, plus a small wobble".

Where the analogy breaks: - The wobble isn't independent per dial. It is a sum of hundreds of products of small secrets (eᵀy − sᵀe₁ …), which is why its distribution can be computed exactly and why failures are correlated with the secret (failure boosting, §9). - The dial isn't read once. Under FO the receiver re-runs the sender's entire computation, so the security argument depends on determinism (r = G(m‖H(ek))), and so do the attacks. - The "noise" partly comes from deliberately throwing away low-order bits (compression). That isn't random noise the sender chose. It is a deterministic rounding error that only behaves like noise.
