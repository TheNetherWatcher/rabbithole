• Before 2014: DRAM disturbance was known as a reliability curiosity.
  • 2014: Kim et al. (ISCA) name Rowhammer and show it is exploitable. They also propose PARA.
  • 2015: Project Zero weaponises it for a kernel privilege escalation. The 2× refresh stopgap ships, and Rowhammer.js appears.
  • 2016–2018: Drammer (mobile) and Throwhammer (network). ECC is still assumed to be safe.
  • 2019: ECCploit breaks ECC.
  • 2020: TRRespass breaks TRR. Revisiting RowHammer quantifies the HC_first collapse. Graphene introduces counting.
  • 2021: U-TRR reverse-engineers TRR. Half-Double shows distance-two disturbance.
  • 2022: Blacksmith flips 40 of 40 DDR4 DIMMs.
  • 2023: RowPress shows that pressing is not hammering. The SAFARI framing paper appears.
  • 2024: ZenHammer reaches AMD and the first DDR5 flip. JEDEC publishes PRAC in JESD79-5C. CoMeT and MOAT appear.
  • 2025–26: GPUHammer targets GPUs and ML. QPRAC, PRACLeak (defense as side channel), and Phoenix (the first full DDR5 exploit, CVE-2025-6202) follow.
