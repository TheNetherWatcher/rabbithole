"Rowhammer flips bits in memory you can't access by hammering an adjacent row; vendors added TRR to fix it."

What that sentence hides:

  1. Why hammering a row disturbs a neighbour at all. It is three distinct physical effects, not "vibration".
  2. TRR is a sampling heuristic with bounded state, and that bound is exactly what every bypass exploits.
  3. The threshold keeps collapsing (139K → 4.8K → projected 128), so a defense that is cheap today is ruinous tomorrow.
  4. Hammering isn't the only trigger. RowPress (holding a row open) and Half-Double (distance two) break the "refresh the immediate neighbour" assumption.
  5. DDR5's real fix is counting (PRAC), not sampling. The 2025–26 literature is busy showing that even counting is expensive and leaky.
