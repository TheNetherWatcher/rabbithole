• Is PRAC enough, and at what cost? Exact counting closes the sampling and undercount holes. But the precharge-time read-modify-write inflates timings (reported up to about 10–18% slowdown), the spec "leaves some questions unanswered", and flips have still been observed on PRAC-capable paths (NDSS 2026 analysis). Verdict: a major advance, not a closed case.
  • The threshold-setting problem. Every counter scheme needs a safe threshold below HC_first. But HC_first keeps falling (139K → 4.8K → about 128 projected). A margin that is safe today may be unsafe at the next node, and setting the threshold low raises false-refresh overhead. No stable safe margin exists.
  • Counting at scale. Exact per-row counters over billions of rows are expensive in area, latency, and energy. Where to store them (in the array, in in-DRAM SRAM, or in the controller) is unsettled.
  • Where should mitigation live? DRAM-only is deployable but has repeatedly failed. The memory controller is one option. OS-cooperative is another. SAFARI's self-managing DRAM and VUSec and MSR's "commodity systems still need software defenses" disagree. There is no consensus.
  • Verification. No accepted method exists to prove a DIMM Rowhammer-safe. TRR was proprietary and unauditable, and "RowHammer-free" labels have proved false. Aging effects are uncharacterised.
  • Supply-chain variation. Vulnerability varies by vendor, die revision, batch, and even within one chip. Patterns don't transfer, which defeats one-size thresholds and certification.
  • Economic misalignment. TRR was optional in JEDEC and shipped as an undocumented black box. VUSec argues that self-interested companies under-invest while end users bear the cost. Standardisation lagged the threat by years.
