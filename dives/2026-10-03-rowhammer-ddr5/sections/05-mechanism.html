Trace one double-sided disturbance on a DDR4 bank. The victim row is V, and the aggressors are A₁ = V−1 and A₂ = V+1.

Row V-1 (A1)  ──┐        wordline toggles raise/lower voltage
Row V    (V)  ──┤ victim  ← coupling + leakage from BOTH sides
Row V+1 (A2)  ──┘

one hammer iteration:
  ACT A1   → wordline A1 high: charge dumped to bitlines, sensed, RESTORED
             (A1's toggle capacitively couples into V's cells, nudges leakage)
  PRE A1   → close A1
  ACT A2   → same from the other side of V
  PRE A2
  … repeat ~4,800× per aggressor before V's next REF …
aggressor (being activated) victim (leaking) idle row
  1. ACT A₁ raises A₁'s wordline. Its cells drive the bitlines, the sense amplifiers latch, and they immediately rewrite A₁ (a destructive read followed by a restore). That is fine so far, because A₁ is intact.
  2. The wordline voltage swing couples into V. Three effects are involved (Kim et al. 2014 §3): (a) capacitive and electromagnetic coupling briefly and partially turns on V's access transistors, bleeding charge; (b) bridges, which are process shorts, offer leakage paths; (c) hot-carrier injection from toggling changes transistor leakiness over time.
  3. PRE A₁, then ACT/PRE A₂. V is now disturbed from the other side too. The disturbance is double-sided because both neighbours' coupling adds.
  4. Each iteration bleeds a sliver of V's charge. V is not being refreshed. Nobody has activated V, and its scheduled REF is up to 64 ms away.
  5. After about 9.6K total activations (the worst DDR4-era chip), V's charge crosses the sense-amp decision point. The next time V is read, the sense amp latches the wrong value. The bit has flipped.

The attacker never issued a single ACT V. Memory integrity was violated purely by activity on V's neighbours.

The RowPress variant (the physics knob)

Instead of fast ACT/PRE cycles, keep A₁ open for a long tAggON. A row held open keeps injecting disturbance without the recovery that a precharge gives. Measured against plain hammering, that is about 17.6× fewer activations at tAggON = 7.8 µs and about 159× fewer at 70.2 µs. A single activation suffices at tAggON ≈ 30 ms (RowPress, ISCA 2023). It is worse when the chip is hot.
