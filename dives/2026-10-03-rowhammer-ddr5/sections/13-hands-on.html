You don't need to hammer anything. Observe the defenses and do the budget arithmetic instead.

  1. Watch the ECC and RAS counters on a real box (safe, read-only): 
    sudo systemctl enable --now rasdaemon
sudo ras-mc-ctl --summary        # memory controller + CE/UE totals
sudo ras-mc-ctl --error-count    # per-DIMM corrected errors
edac-util -v
    A rising CE count on one DIMM is the observable fingerprint of marginal cells.
  2. Compute your own activation budget. Pick your DIMM's tRC (for example 46 ns) and tREFW (32 ms on DDR5). The budget is about tREFW / tRC. Divide it by a plausible HC_first, say 4800. The quotient is how many independent victims one refresh window could, in principle, threaten. Watch it balloon as you plug in the projected HC_first of 128. The slider in the math section does this for you.
  3. Reason through PARA. With p = 0.002 and N = 50,000 hammers, Pmiss = (1 − 0.001)50000 ≈ e−50 ≈ 2×10⁻²². Now set N, the hammers you must survive, to match HC_first = 128. Ask what p you would need for the same safety, and what that does to overhead.
  4. (Optional, your own hardware only) Memtest86 test 13 runs a Rowhammer check from a bootable stick.
