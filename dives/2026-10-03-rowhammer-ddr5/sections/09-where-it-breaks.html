TRR: bounded tracking. A tracker that remembers N rows is beaten by N+1 aggressors.

  • TRRespass (S&P 2020): many-sided hammering, with up to about 19 aggressors, overflowed the trackers. It flipped bits on 13 of 42 DDR4 DIMMs across all three vendors (paper).
  • U-TRR (MICRO 2021): used retention failures as a side channel to see exactly when and how many rows TRR refreshes. It bypassed TRR on 45 modules, with up to 99.9% of rows flippable (arXiv).
  • Blacksmith (S&P 2022): non-uniform frequency and phase patterns flipped all 40 of 40 DDR4 DIMMs.

DDR5 TRR: Phoenix (S&P 2026, CVE-2025-6202). SK Hynix's DDR5 TRR samples over a long, complex schedule. The best bypass pattern spanned 128 REF intervals, about 8× the roughly 16 assumed. "Self-correcting synchronisation" re-aligns the pattern after missed refreshes, so the hammers land in the sampler's blind spots. It flipped bits on 15 of 15 SK Hynix DDR5 DIMMs (made January 2021 to December 2024). In the ETH COMSEC results, the first flip came at about 109 s, and root on a default DDR5 desktop followed in about 5 minutes. On-die ECC did not help (ETH COMSEC).

The physics breaks the "refresh the neighbour" assumption too.

  • Half-Double (Google, 2021): disturbance reaches distance two. Heavy access to row A plus a few accesses to the intermediate row B flips row C. TRR's own refresh of B acts as an access that propagates the disturbance. This is an intrinsic silicon property that widens as geometry shrinks (Google).
  • RowPress (ISCA 2023): pressing a row, not hammering it. This is orthogonal to activation-count defenses.
Misconceptions practitioners hold (each one is wrong):
  • "ECC fixes it." ECCploit (S&P 2019) crafts multi-flip words and uses correction timing.
  • "DDR5 is immune." ZenHammer and Phoenix say otherwise.
  • "Server or ECC memory is safe." ECCploit targeted server ECC, and ZenHammer hit AMD server and desktop parts.
  • "It needs physical access." Project Zero did it in 2015 from a sandboxed userland program. Rowhammer.js did it from JavaScript.
  • "TRR solved it." Every bypass above says otherwise.
