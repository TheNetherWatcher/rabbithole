1.36Mactivations one bank can take in one 64 ms refresh window: the attacker's whole budget

Software's bedrock assumption is that memory you cannot name, you cannot change. DRAM scaling broke it. By the 20-nm node, the charge separating "1" from "0" and the spacing between rows had both shrunk to where normal-rate activations of one row leak a neighbour past its margin inside a single 64 ms refresh window.

The constraint that forces every design decision is the activation budget against HC_first. A single bank admits at most about tREFW / tRC ≈ 64 ms / 47 ns ≈ 1.36M activations per refresh window. This is arithmetic, not a paper figure. If HC_first is 139K (DDR3, 2014), you need about 10% of that budget aimed at one victim. That is hard but doable. If HC_first is 4.8K (LPDDR4, 2020), it is trivial. Projected future chips sit near 128. The defense problem is: catch an over-activated row before a threshold that is shrinking out from under you, cheaply, across billions of rows.

Concrete generation trend (Revisiting RowHammer, ISCA 2020, 1580 chips):

GenerationHC_first (old node → new node)
DDR369.2K → 22.4K
DDR417.5K → 10K
LPDDR416.8K → 4.8K

The minimum observed overall was 9.6K total activations (4.8K to each of two aggressors, double-sided). Bit flips per fixed activation count rose about 500× over the same span.
