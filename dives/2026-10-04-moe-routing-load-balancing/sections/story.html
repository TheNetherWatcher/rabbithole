Four experts and 1,000 tokens, each token sent to its top 2. That is 2,000 picks, so a fair router gives each expert 500. The dashed line marks that share.

The naive way. With no balancing, the same favourites keep winning. In one traced batch, expert 0 takes 820 of the 2,000 picks. In a 16-expert toy run with no balancing, MaxVio stays at 2.57.

The key idea. Give each expert a bias that only changes which experts get picked. Hot experts get a negative bias and idle ones a positive bias. The bias is controller state, not a learned weight.

Selection, then gate. After its bias, expert 0 drops to 0.569 and expert 1 rises to 0.649. This token picks experts 3 and 1 instead of 0 and 3. Its gate weights still come from the raw scores.

The update. After the batch, each bias moves by a fixed step of 0.001, using only the sign of its load error. Expert 0 goes from −0.20 to −0.201. No gradient is involved.

Repeat. Run the update for 400 steps and the loads settle near the fair share. In the toy run with u = 0.001, MaxVio averages 0.084 over the last 100 steps.

The trade-off. The step size sets speed against jitter. At u = 0.05 the bias overshoots and never settles, with MaxVio 0.492 over the last 100 steps.
